## 1. Executive assessment

This is a serious, technically substantial candidate for publication. Its principal claim is the exclusion of the **entire closed two-parameter Szöllősi family** from quadruplets of mutually unbiased bases in dimension six, including degenerate parameters. The strongest feature is the separation between an untrusted numerical generator and a substantially smaller interval-arithmetic checker. The hull-based argument also avoids requiring isolated, simple or finitely many unbiased-vector solutions.

My audit found substantial positive evidence. All eleven supplied checker test groups passed. I freshly recomputed the graph conditions for **all 16,593 parameter certificates**, independently checked the Fourier symmetry witnesses and fundamental-domain tiling, and freshly replayed phase-space coverage for seven selected parent certificates, including one requiring subdivision to level 12.

Two important issues remain. First, the formal coverage statements use closed boxes, while the implementation relies on canonical half-open ownership; some stated lemmas are therefore false as written. Second, the replay driver accepts cached success records without binding them to the current certificate and checker inputs. I reproduced this acceptance weakness.

**Neither finding demonstrates a false exclusion theorem or an actual missing parameter region.** Both appear repairable without redesigning the research. Nevertheless, they concern the central certification claim and should be corrected before acceptance. My present assessment is upper 3*, not yet a secure 4*.

## 3. Contribution and positioning

### The strongest defensible contribution

The manuscript’s principal contribution is a certificate-supported, uniform non-extension theorem for a complete continuous family:

\[
S=\begin{pmatrix}A&B\\ B^*&-A^*\end{pmatrix},
\]

where \(A,B\) are the specified unimodular circulant blocks and \(S\) satisfies the Hadamard condition.

The intended result excludes not just generic parameters or sampled matrices, but the **closed family**, including repeated-root boundary cases. That distinction is mathematically important.

The second contribution is methodological: cover all possible unbiased vectors by phase-space hulls, certify separation within each hull, and rule out the graph configuration that two additional unbiased bases would require. The argument does not depend on a numerical root count being complete.

The Fourier-family result has a different status. Jaming and colleagues already proved that the standard basis and any member of the Fourier family cannot extend to four mutually unbiased bases. Here the contribution is a new checked implementation and verification framework, **not a second new exclusion theorem**. The manuscript largely recognises that distinction. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="0"}

### Importance within the current research programme

Matolcsi, Matszangosz, Varga and Weiner’s 2026 paper explicitly formulates the Szöllősi-family exclusion as Conjecture 3. Their Proposition 4.2 combines it with their algebraic Conjecture 2 to obtain the three-basis maximum in dimension six. Thus, the present result would remove a genuine, explicitly identified obstacle—but would **not by itself prove Zauner’s conjecture**. The manuscript’s conditional corollary is appropriately framed. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="1"}

My significance judgement is therefore: **a substantial specialist advance, with a potentially influential certification method**. It should not be marketed as the resolution of the full dimension-six problem.

## 4. Major comments

### 4.1 The coverage theorem must distinguish half-open ownership from closed interval enclosures

**Issue.** The implementation appears to use a sound *ownership* convention, but the manuscript repeatedly states a stronger, false closed-box property.

**Manuscript evidence.** Theorem 7(O1) requires the selector to discard only boxes disjoint from the fundamental domain \(\mathcal F\). Lemma 11 states this directly, and its proof expressly refers to the **closed** box. Section 6.5 then uses this assertion in the coverage and subdivision argument.

The ordering selector instead exploits half-open dyadic cells. At a shared boundary, it may discard one closed cell containing a fundamental-domain point while keeping that point’s canonical owner.

This is not merely a hypothetical possibility. Consider

\[
t=\frac{\pi}{32},\qquad
u=v=-e^{it},\qquad w=e^{-2it},
\]
\[
\alpha=2e^{it}-e^{-2it}.
\]

Choose the ordered unit roots \(x,y,z\) of the defining cubic for \(\alpha\). These give a valid fundamental-domain point. My additional interval calculation brackets the \(x,y\) roots and verifies the angular-sector inequalities.

At parameter level 9, the checker:

- **discards** the closed cell with indices \((34,106,264,263)\);
- **retains** the canonical owner \((34,106,264,264)\).

The point has \(u=v\) at grid coordinate 264, so it lies on the upper \(v\)-boundary of the discarded closed cell.

**Why it matters.** This disproves Lemma 11’s closed-box statement as written. It does **not** show an uncovered point: the correct owner is retained. Nevertheless, the formal bridge from the implementation to the theorem needs repair.

There is a second mismatch. Theorem 7(O2) demands coverage for every

\[
p\in P\cap\Sigma,
\]

whereas the parameter-subdivision discussion verifies only the fundamental-domain portion. Moreover, its assertion that every point of \(\mathcal F\cap P\) has a retained descendant *inside that same closed parent* is too strong at ownership boundaries.

**Required revision.** Define half-open ownership cells \(Q_{\ell,i}\), with closed enclosures

\[
P_{\ell,i}=\overline{Q_{\ell,i}}
\]

used for interval arithmetic. State that every \(p\in\mathcal F\) has a retained canonical owner at every relevant level. Require coverage on

\[
p\in\mathcal F\cap Q_{\ell,i},
\]

and prove subdivision by following that unique ownership path.

Simply replacing \(P\cap\Sigma\) by \(P\cap\mathcal F\) is insufficient unless the ownership issue is also addressed. The existing certificates may remain usable unchanged.

### 4.2 Cached success is not bound to the current proof inputs

**Issue.** The replay driver’s resumption mechanism can report complete verification of changed certificate data without checking the changes.

**Code evidence.** In `chk/run_check.py`, previously successful boxes are loaded into a dictionary keyed only by

\[
(\text{level},\text{indices}).
\]

When a key is present, the current box is skipped. The driver does not require agreement between the cached result and the current hull digest, family, table, checker version or compiled kernel. The final summary can consequently associate current hashes with statuses inherited from a different input.

**Reproduced regression.** I made a temporary copy of the Fourier certificate input and replaced the hull list for the level-9 origin certificate with an empty list. I then supplied the original positive replay cache to the actual driver, without a verification-limit option.

It reported:

| Output field | Result |
|---|---:|
| Changed certificates | 1 |
| `verified_ok` | 1,935 |
| `cover_complete` | `true` |
| Exit code | 0 |

The changed certificate was not reverified.

**Why it matters.** A checker advertised as accepting generator output only as untrusted data must not allow an old status bit to substitute for checking different data. This is particularly relevant to a package assembled from resumed and merged runs.

The qualification is essential: **I found no such mismatch in the submitted certificate set.** All actual hull digests matched, the inspected summary source hashes matched the supplied sources, and the fresh checks passed. This is a demonstrated weakness in the verification workflow, not evidence of fabricated or corrupted results.

**Required revision.** Make cached results content-addressed. Bind each success to full hashes of the family definition, parameter box, hull data, table, relevant checker and subdivision code, and build identity. Reject mismatches and provide an explicit clean-output proof mode. Record or disallow the `MUB6_KERNEL` override in that mode.

Add the regression above and retain a clean, fully replayed record set with a sealed manifest.

### 4.3 Section 11.4 overstates demonstrated transferability

**Issue.** The claim that the Karlsson three-parameter and Szöllősi four-parameter families require changes only to input data and perhaps the selector is not established.

**Manuscript and code evidence.** The implemented interface uses entries of the form

\[
c\,e^{i\langle E,p\rangle}
\]

and the implemented family-constraint structure. The kernel has fixed capacities, including `MAXP=4`, `MAXT=8` and `MAXN=7`. The manuscript supplies no complete encoding of either larger family satisfying this interface.

Szöllősi’s four-parameter construction involves algebraic reconstruction, rational companion expressions and branch choices. A four-parameter description does not automatically make every matrix entry a character of a four-torus. An enlarged phase encoding might be possible, but it would require additional variables, constraints or branch-coverage arguments that are not supplied here. :chatgpt-content-reference{index="2"}

**Why it matters.** Transferability contributes directly to the paper’s methodological significance and prospective REF case. A demonstrated two-family framework should not be credited as an already established general solution for substantially larger families.

**Required revision.** Either provide complete encodings and verify the interface assumptions, including exceptional branches and capacity requirements, or recast these extensions as research directions. This is not an objection to either of the two exclusions actually checked.

## 5. Rigour, results and inference

### The central mathematical reduction is convincing

The phase normalisation and scaling are consistent. For unscaled unimodular vectors \(v,w\), orthogonality and mutual unbiasedness correspond respectively to

\[
|\langle v,w\rangle|^2=0
\quad\text{and}\quad
|\langle v,w\rangle|^2=6.
\]

A hypothetical pair of additional bases supplies twelve vectors. The within-hull separation condition prevents two of these vectors from occupying the same hull. Their hulls must therefore give two six-cliques in the possible-orthogonality graph, with every cross-pair present in the possible-unbiasedness graph.

This implication remains valid when hulls overlap or contain clusters. Extra possible edges make exclusion harder, not easier. **There is no need to prove that each hull contains exactly one root.** This is one of the strongest aspects of the paper.

The parametric Krawczyk argument also correctly permits a numerically chosen finite preconditioner: it need not be a certified inverse. The relevant requirement is that the resulting enclosure is valid. A poor preconditioner should lead to failure to exclude, rather than a false exclusion.

## 7. Minor and secondary comments

### Appendix A contains an invalid intermediate induction

The proof of Lemma R claims an error exponent \(c+8\) at every expression-tree node. Multiplication combines the uncertainty in both operands, so this induction is not valid.

For example, take

\[
\widehat a_1=\widehat a_2=1,\qquad
a_1=a_2=(1-u)^{-8},
\]

and multiply them. The exact result is \((1-u)^{-16}\), whereas the claimed one-operation induction gives essentially \((1-u)^{-9}\). The attached exact rational check confirms the strict violation even with the stated small additive allowance.

**This does not refute Lemma R’s final, generously padded bound.** It refutes the proof step offered for it. Repair the propagation argument or remove the unused assurance claim. Since the generator lies outside the proof’s trusted components, this is not a counterexample to Theorem 1.

### Mutation-test claims need qualification

Section 1.3 says the mutation tests exercise each soundness ingredient; Section 8.3 reports a surviving mutation involving the mixed-parameter term. These statements should agree.

The term is present in the current kernel, and I found no associated current-code counterexample. Nevertheless, the suggestion that a test cannot reasonably expose its omission is too strong. The audit supplies a simple exact, generic Krawczyk assembly example that distinguishes inclusion of the mixed term from its omission. It is a proposed unit-test fixture, not a counterexample to this kernel.

### Subdivision depth and references

Section 6.5 describes subdivision to \(\ell+1\) and \(\ell+2\), but eight recorded certificates required \(\ell+3\). Update the prose. Correct Theorem 7’s reference to nonexistent Section 6.6 and reconcile stale lemma numbers in the exact-script names and documentation.

### Historical-code comparisons

Where a claimed improvement depends on a particular tolerance or arithmetic choice in an earlier implementation, give an identifiable source snapshot or precise archived reference. The distinction between “the published theorem”, “the available historical program” and “this new certificate format” should remain explicit.

### Root counts and proof counts

Maintain the manuscript’s generally good distinction between numerical root discoveries and certified hull coverage. Neither the number of hulls nor the number of converged numerical solutions is, by itself, an exact root count.

## 8. Prioritised revision plan

**Must fix before publication:** Rewrite the ownership and coverage statements coherently; bind cached verification to current proof inputs; add the negative cache regression and retain a clean replay record; narrow or substantiate Section 11.4; repair or remove the invalid Appendix A induction.

**Should fix to strengthen the paper:** Make the verified boundary-ownership example a permanent regression; add a targeted mixed-parameter assembly test; update all subdivision-depth descriptions; distinguish complete supplied replays from selected third-party replays; provide precise provenance for comparisons with historical code.

**Could improve presentation or future work:** Consolidate the certificate specification into a short, self-contained interface document, archive a stable release, and demonstrate a genuinely new family encoding. A proof-assistant implementation would be valuable, but should not be presented as necessary for the present result to be publishable.

## 9. Editorial recommendation

**Major revisions.**

The recommendation rests on defects in the stated mathematical interface and verification workflow, not on a demonstrated failure of the exclusion result. The likely repairs are bounded: they do not presently require a new research strategy or a replacement certificate generator.

My confidence is **high** in the identified boundary and cache findings, and **moderately high** in the assessment that the central theorem is salvageable with the existing approach.
